# behaviour | 4.1.2 | Individual economic decision making
Behavioural economics :: Studies how people actually make decisions, which may not be rational due to bounded rationality, bounded self-control, biases and the influence of others.
Bounded rationality :: People make decisions with limited information, time and ability to process it, so they satisfice rather than maximise.
Biases in decision making :: Rules of thumb (heuristics), anchoring, availability bias, and social norms influence choices.
Altruism and perceptions of fairness :: People may act to benefit others or reject unfair offers even when it costs them, as shown by the ultimatum game.
Choice architecture and nudges :: Designing choices to influence behaviour, e.g. default options such as automatic pension enrolment, restricted choice and mandated choice.
Imperfect information :: Asymmetric information leads to poor decisions, e.g. in second-hand car markets (the 'lemons' problem) and health care.
Utility theory :: Rational consumers aim to maximise utility. Marginal utility diminishes as more of a good is consumed.
