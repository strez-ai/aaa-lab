# finance | 2.1-2.3 | Theme 2: Managing business activities, finance
+ bus-aqa:finance
Business plans :: Set out objectives, finance, marketing and operations; help secure funding and plan, but may be inaccurate.
Liquidity :: The ability to pay short-term debts. Measured by the current ratio and the acid test ratio ((current assets - inventory) ÷ current liabilities).
Business failure :: Internal causes (poor cash flow, management) and external causes (economic downturn, competition).

# environment | 2.5 | Theme 2: External influences
Effects of economic factors :: Inflation, exchange rates, interest rates, taxation, government spending and the business cycle affect demand and costs.
Legislation :: Consumer protection, employment protection, environmental protection and competition policy constrain businesses.
Unemployment and business :: High unemployment lowers demand but makes recruiting easier and keeps wage costs down.

+ bus-aqa:strategic-position
+ bus-aqa:strategic-direction

Corporate social responsibility :: Taking account of the impact of decisions on stakeholders and the environment beyond legal requirements. Carroll's CSR pyramid: economic, legal, ethical, philanthropic.
Kaplan and Norton's balanced scorecard :: Measures performance using financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth perspectives.
Elkington's triple bottom line :: Profit, people and planet.
# global | 4.1-4.5 | Theme 4: Global business
Globalisation and trade :: Reduced trade barriers, improved transport and communication, and the growth of emerging economies increase global trade.
Protectionism :: Tariffs, quotas, domestic subsidies and legislation protect home industries but can reduce efficiency and provoke retaliation.
Trading blocs :: Groups of countries with reduced trade barriers between them, e.g. the EU, ASEAN and the USMCA.
Assessing a country as a market :: Consider income levels, ease of doing business, infrastructure, political stability and exchange rates.
Assessing a production location :: Costs of production, skills, infrastructure, government incentives and ethical considerations.
Reasons for global mergers and joint ventures :: Spread risk, enter new markets, acquire brands and resources, and gain economies of scale.
Global competitiveness :: Achieved through cost competitiveness or differentiation, and affected by exchange rates.
Global marketing strategies :: Ethnocentric, polycentric and geocentric approaches; Ansoff's matrix in an international context; glocalisation.
Niche and cultural factors :: Cultural differences in language, customs and tastes require businesses to adapt products and promotion.
Multinational corporations :: Impacts on host countries: jobs, investment and technology but also exploitation and profit repatriation. Controlled by legislation, pressure groups and social media.
Ethics in global business :: Stakeholder conflicts over pay, working conditions, environmental damage and supply chain ethics.
Transfer pricing and tax avoidance :: MNCs may shift profits to low-tax countries, which raises ethical concerns.
