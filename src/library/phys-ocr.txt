# motion | 3.1-3.2 | Motion and forces
+ phys-aqa:mechanics
Drag :: Depends on speed, shape and the medium. Objects falling through fluids reach terminal velocity when drag equals weight.
Archimedes' principle :: Upthrust on an object equals the weight of the fluid it displaces.
Pressure in fluids :: p = hρg.
Stopping distance :: Thinking distance + braking distance. Reaction time affects thinking distance; speed, road and tyres affect braking distance.
# energy | 3.3-3.5 | Work, energy, materials and momentum
+ phys-aqa:materials
Elastic potential energy :: E = ½kx² for a material obeying Hooke's law.
Ductile and brittle :: Ductile materials (copper) can be drawn into wires; brittle materials (glass) break without plastic deformation. Polymeric materials such as rubber show hysteresis.
# electricity | 4.1-4.3 | Charge, current and circuits
+ phys-aqa:electricity
Mean drift velocity :: I = Anev, where n is the number density of charge carriers. Conductors have high n; semiconductors low n.
Kilowatt-hour :: A unit of energy equal to 3.6 MJ. Cost = energy in kWh × cost per kWh.
Light-emitting diodes and Planck's constant :: LEDs emit light when the pd exceeds a threshold; eV = hc/λ allows h to be estimated.
# waves | 4.4 | Waves
+ phys-aqa:waves

Intensity of a wave :: I = P/A; intensity ∝ amplitude².
Malus' law :: For polarised light through a polariser: I = I0 cos²θ.

# astro | 5.5 | Astrophysics and cosmology
+ phys-aqa:option-astro
Formation of a star :: Gravitational collapse of dust and gas forms a protostar; nuclear fusion starts when the core is hot enough.
Life cycle of stars :: Low-mass stars become red giants then white dwarfs; high-mass stars become red supergiants, explode as supernovae and leave neutron stars or black holes.
Chandrasekhar limit :: About 1.44 solar masses: the maximum mass of a white dwarf, supported by electron degeneracy pressure.
Energy levels and spectra in stars :: Emission and absorption line spectra identify elements in stars.
Astronomical distances :: Astronomical unit, light-year and parsec. Stellar parallax: d (pc) = 1/p (arcseconds).
Cosmological principle :: On a large scale the universe is homogeneous and isotropic, and the laws of physics are universal.
Evidence for the Big Bang :: Hubble's law (galaxies receding) and the cosmic microwave background radiation at about 2.7 K.
Dark matter and dark energy :: Dark matter explains galaxy rotation curves; dark energy explains the accelerating expansion of the universe.

# medical | 6.5 | Medical imaging
X-ray production :: Electrons accelerated by a high pd hit a metal target, producing X-ray photons (bremsstrahlung).
X-ray attenuation :: I = I0e^(-μx). Mechanisms: simple scatter, photoelectric effect, Compton scattering and pair production.
Contrast media :: Substances such as barium or iodine with high attenuation make soft tissues visible in X-ray images.
CAT scans :: A rotating X-ray tube and detectors build a 3D image from many slices. More detailed than X-rays but a higher radiation dose.
Medical tracers :: Radioactive substances such as technetium-99m and fluorine-18, detected by gamma cameras.
Gamma camera :: Collimator, scintillator and photomultiplier tubes produce an image from gamma photons emitted by a tracer.
PET scans :: Fluorine-18 emits positrons, which annihilate with electrons to produce two gamma photons in opposite directions, detected to locate the source.
Ultrasound :: Sound above 20 kHz produced by piezoelectric transducers. Reflections at tissue boundaries create images; A-scans and B-scans.
Acoustic impedance :: Z = ρc. Reflected intensity ratio = (Z2 - Z1)² ÷ (Z2 + Z1)². Coupling gel avoids almost total reflection at the skin.
Doppler imaging :: The Doppler effect in ultrasound measures the speed of blood flow.
